Saying who you are

Men talyp

This unit is about people: the words for I, you, he and she, how to say what someone is (a student, tired, Turkmen) and where they are from. It also completes vowel harmony with rounding, and introduces the shorthand the guides use from now on for endings that change.

01High vowels and rounding: y, i, u, ü

The endings of A1 unit 2 had a or e. Many other endings have a high vowel instead: y, i, u or ü. To choose, ask the question of unit 2 first: after a back vowel the ending has y, after a front vowel i. In this unit you will meet -dyr and -dir, and -ly and -li, as in aşgabatly (from Ashgabat) and Tejenli (from Tejen).

Then comes rounding. The high vowel becomes u or ü, the rounded vowels, in just one place: when it is the vowel of the second syllable of the word, the first syllable has o, ö, u or ü, and it is not the last letter of the word. Everywhere else it stays y or i.

One ending, three spellings
WordMeaningWhy
gözümmy eyesecond syllable, after ö: rounded ü
gözihis or her eyethe last letter of the word: no rounding
dostummy friendsecond syllable, after o: rounded u
dostyhis or her friendthe last letter: y
öýümizour houseü in the second syllable; i in the third
kitabymmy bookthe first syllable has i: no rounding
Examples
  • gözümmy eye
  • öýümmy house
  • öýihis or her house
  • Ol türkmendir.He is Turkmen.front e: -dir
  • Ol lukmandyr.She is a doctor.back a: -dyr

The words for my and his come in A1 unit 9; here they only show the pattern. You will see it on many endings, so it pays to notice it now.

The shorthand. From now on, the guides write the changing vowel of an ending as a capital letter. A is a or e. Y is y or i, or u or ü where rounding applies. So the plural is -lAr (-lar, -ler), the ending in aşgabatly is -lY (-ly, -li…), and the ending you meet in this unit is -dYr (-dyr, -dir…).

The shorthand
A  =  a after a back vowel, e after a front vowel
Y  =  y after a back vowel, i after a front vowel
Y  =  u or ü in the second syllable after o, ö, u or ü, unless it ends the word
Watch out

Rounding never touches A: gözler (eyes), öýler (houses), never gözlör. And a vowel at the very end of a word stays unrounded: gözi, not gözü.

02I, you, he, she: the personal pronouns

PronounMeaning
menI
senyou (one person you know well)
olhe, she, it
bizwe
sizyou (several people, or one person, politely)
olarthey

There is no gender: ol means he, she or it, and the context tells you which. ol and olar are the same words as that and those in A1 unit 2: ol kitap is that book, Ol mugallym is he or she is a teacher.

sen or siz? Use sen with a friend, a child, or a relative of your own age or younger. Use siz with anyone older, with people you don't know well, at work, and with several people. When in doubt, use siz: being too polite is rarely a mistake. If someone calls you sen, that doesn't mean you should answer with sen.

Examples
  • Men talyp.I am a student.
  • Sen mugallym.You are a teacher.
  • Ol lukman.He is a doctor. / She is a doctor.
  • Biz okuwçy.We are pupils.
  • Siz sürüji.You are a driver.
  • Olar talyp.They are students.

When the pronoun stays. In the short form of to be (next section), nothing else in the sentence shows who is meant, so the pronoun has to be there: Men talyp is I am a student, but Talyp. on its own is just “a student”. When a word carries a personal ending, as verbs do from A1 unit 6, the pronoun can be left out.

Tip

Notice that the noun after biz, siz or olar can stay singular: Biz okuwçy, we are pupils. After olar you will also hear the plural: Olar talyplar.

03To be: Men talyp

In the present, Turkmen usually says to be with no word at all. Put the subject and what it is side by side, whether that is a job, a nationality or a feeling. This short form works in every person, and it is the one you will hear most:

Examples
  • Men ýadaw.I'm tired.
  • Sen aç.You're hungry.
  • Biz bagtly.We're happy.
  • Men türkmen.I'm Turkmen.

-dYr in the third person. In careful speech and in writing, ol and olar often add -dYr (-dyr, -dir, -dur, -dür). It makes the statement sound firmer, a little like “she is indeed a teacher”, and it is common in written facts and descriptions. With olar it is -dYr or -dYrlAr.

Examples
  • Ol mugallymdyr.She is a teacher.
  • Ol keseldir.He is ill.
  • Bu kitap täzedir.This book is new.
  • Olar lukmandyrlar.They are doctors.

The formal written form. In formal writing, letters and speeches, you will also see -dYr followed by a personal ending in the first and second persons: Men talypdyryn (I am a student), Biz minnetdardyrys (we are grateful). You don't need these forms to speak, but you should recognize them. The endings are the ones the present tense of verbs uses in A1 unit 6:

Personal endings after -dYr
men   -Yn       talypdyryn,  türkmendirin
sen   -sYň      talypdyrsyň,  türkmendirsiň
biz   -Ys       talypdyrys,  türkmendiris
siz   -sYňYz    talypdyrsyňyz,  türkmendirsiňiz
olar  -lAr      talypdyrlar,  türkmendirler
Watch out

Keep the subject

With the short form, the pronoun or name must be there: Men talyp, never just Talyp for I am a student. With an ending, it can go: Talypdyryn already says I.

Tip

No bir with jobs

Say Men mugallym (I am a teacher), without bir. Not and questions with to be (Men talyp däl, Sen talypmy?) come in A1 unit 4.

04Nationalities and where you are from

Nationality words are nouns, and they are written in lowercase: türkmen (a Turkmen), rus (a Russian), iňlis (an English person), nemes (a German), fransuz (a French person), özbek (an Uzbek), gazak (a Kazakh). Use them like jobs, with the short form of to be: Men türkmen, I am Turkmen.

From a town. To ask where someone is from, say Siz nireli? (or Sen nireli?). Answer with the name of your town and the ending -lY (-ly, -li): Men aşgabatly, I am from Ashgabat. The word takes a small letter, since it is no longer a name. Use it as a set pattern for now; the ending itself is taught in A2 unit 12.

Examples
  • Siz nireli?Where are you from?
  • Men aşgabatly.I'm from Ashgabat.back a: -ly
  • Ol tejenli.She's from Tejen.front e: -li
  • Biz daşoguzly.We're from Dashoguz.third syllable: no rounding
  • Men maryly.I'm from Mary.
Watch out

Nationality words take no capital: türkmen, iňlis. Names of countries and towns do: Türkmenistan, Angliýa, Aşgabat.

Summary

  1. Endings with a high vowel have y after back vowels and i after front ones; u or ü only in the second syllable after o, ö, u, ü, and never at the end of a word: gözüm, but gözi.
  2. From now on, A stands for a/e and Y for y/i/u/ü: -lAr, -dYr, -lY.
  3. The pronouns are men, sen, ol, biz, siz, olar. ol is he, she and it; siz is plural, and polite for one person.
  4. In the present, to be usually has no word: Men talyp. The pronoun must then be said.
  5. In careful style the third person adds -dYr: Ol mugallymdyr. Formal writing adds -dYr with an ending in every person: Men talypdyryn.
  6. Nationality words are lowercase nouns: Men türkmen. Siz nireli? asks where someone is from: Men aşgabatly.
